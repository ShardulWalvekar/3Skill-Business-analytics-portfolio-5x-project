## Project 3: Supply Chain and Inventory Analysis

**Folder:** [`3skillprj3/`](3skillprj3/)

### Objective
Use inventory, supplier, product-cost, and delivery data to assess stock risk and identify ways to improve inventory planning and operational efficiency.

### Analysis included
- Total units sold, revenue, and estimated profit
- Stock quantity compared with reorder levels
- Understock, overstock, and optimal inventory classifications
- Fast-moving versus slow-moving products
- Supplier delivery-time comparisons
- Product profitability and top-selling products

### SQL work
The SQL script demonstrates:
- Creating a database and inventory table
- Removing exact duplicate records with `SELECT DISTINCT`
- Replacing negative stock and units-sold values with zero
- Calculating delivery duration
- Aggregating revenue and profit
- Classifying inventory and product movement
- Ranking products and comparing supplier delivery times

**Important:** Replacing negative values with zero is one implemented cleaning rule, not a universally correct treatment. In a production workflow, investigate the source of invalid values and document the chosen handling method.

### Business takeaways from the supplied report
- The report flags an overstock classification across categories; validate the underlying business rule and source data before acting on it.
- Shipping time is reported as broadly consistent across categories.
- The summary recommends investigating slow-moving inventory and prioritizing decisions using both margin and stock movement.

### Files
- Power BI report: [`Supply Chain & Inventory Analysis.pbix`](3skillprj3/Supply%20Chain%20%26%20Inventory%20Analysis.pbix)
- SQL analysis: [`Analysis_prj3.sql`](3skillprj3/Analysis_prj3.sql)

## Project 4: Retail Inventory and Demand Forecasting

**Folder:** [`3skill_final_prj4/`](3skill_final_prj4/)

### Objective
Explore retail demand, sales patterns, seasonality, inventory levels, and forecasted demand to support better replenishment and planning decisions.

### Reported highlights
- Total sales revenue: approximately **$550M**
- Units sold: approximately **10M**
- Demand forecast: approximately **10M**
- The summary describes revenue as relatively balanced across four regions.

### Analysis included
- Sales by region and product category
- Seasonal performance comparisons
- Actual units sold versus demand forecast
- Inventory levels, discounts, and pricing context
- Forecast-oriented views for planning

### Business takeaways and validation
- The report indicates relatively balanced revenue across regions and categories.
- Actual units sold and forecasted demand are reported at similar aggregate levels.
- Aggregate similarity alone does **not** establish forecast accuracy. A stronger evaluation should compare forecasts with actual outcomes at the same time and product level using measures such as MAE, RMSE, or WAPE, ideally on a held-out period.

### Files
- Power BI report: [`Retail Inventory & Demand Forecasting Analysis.pbix`](3skill_final_prj4/Retail%20Inventory%20%26%20Demand%20Forecasting%20Analysis.pbix)
- Dataset: [`retail_store_inventory.csv`](3skill_final_prj4/retail_store_inventory.csv)

## How to Explore the Projects

1. Open a project folder and read its PDF summary or documentation first.
2. Review the CSV dataset to understand the available fields and grain of the data.
3. Open the `.pbix` file in **Microsoft Power BI Desktop** to explore the report interactively.
4. Review `Analysis_prj3.sql` in a compatible SQL client to understand the supply-chain queries.
5. Compare the reported findings with the underlying data before using them to make business decisions.

### Requirements
- Microsoft Power BI Desktop for `.pbix` files
- A SQL environment compatible with the syntax used in `Analysis_prj3.sql` (the script uses MySQL-style syntax)
- Excel, Python, or another CSV-compatible tool for inspecting datasets
